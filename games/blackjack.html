How Hi-Lo works

Each 2 through 6 counts +1, each 7, 8, 9 counts 0, and each 10, J, Q, K, A counts −1. A fresh shoe is balanced, so a positive running count means the low cards are gone and the tens and aces are still coming — good for you, because blackjacks pay 3:2 and the dealer busts more often standing on stiffs.

Divide the running count by the decks still in the shoe and you get the true count, which is the number that actually matters: +6 with six decks left is barely anything, +6 with one deck left is a different game.

The best-known count-based deviations from basic strategy: take insurance at true count ≥ +3, stand on 16 against a ten at ≥ 0, stand on 15 against a ten at ≥ +4, and stand on 12 against a 3 at ≥ +2. This trainer grades you on plain basic strategy plus the insurance decision — the rest are listed so you know they exist.
